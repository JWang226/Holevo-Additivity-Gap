# Comparison and reduction

The channel's adjoint is represented by a polynomial in its unitary family. In the free regular model, the [Collins–Youn bound](lean:Nonadditivity.CollinsYounProduct.collinsYounBound) controls that polynomial by a Hilbert–Schmidt coefficient norm with scale $c(K,n)$.

To make one finite sample work for all observables, reduce the sphere of normalized traceless Hermitian matrices to a finite net. Encode the net tests into a common polynomial, use two-generator word reductions, and linearize. These reductions keep coefficient sizes and approximation losses explicit.

Finite Haar moment estimates then compare the reduced polynomial with its free model. They supply a quantitative expectation bound rather than leaving a generic norm-convergence assertion as a premise.

Read [free operators and finite Haar matrices](concept:free-haar), [the net certificate](concept:adjoint-certificate), and [universal factorization](result:universal-factorization). The net reduction is [exists_net_linearization](lean:Nonadditivity.StructuredLinearization.exists_net_linearization).
