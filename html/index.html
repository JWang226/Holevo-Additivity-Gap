Quantum communication · mathematics and Lean proofs

How much could entanglement help in classical communication

Finite quantum channels can have arbitrarily small single-use Holevo information and arbitrarily large information per use when inputs are entangled across uses.

This guide explains Jinzhao Wang’s Holevo additivity-gap construction, its scaling, and its operational meaning. Each explanation leads to the corresponding formal statements.

Three quantities to keep apart

\chi(T)Single-use Holevo information: optimize over ensembles sent through one channel.

\tfrac12\chi(T\otimes T)Two-use information per use: allow entangled inputs to a pair of uses.

C(T)Operational capacity: the rate of physical codes with vanishing decoding error.

How these quantities are related →

The central separation

For every positive tolerance and every target gain and ratio, one finite channel can satisfy all three conditions:

0<\chi(T)\le\varepsilon,\qquad C(T)-\chi(T)\ge A,\qquad \frac{\chi(T\otimes T)}{2\chi(T)}\ge R.

The channel may depend on the targets. Its input and output spaces are finite, and their dimensions grow along the constructed families. Small positive single-use information with large capacity gain and two-use ratio explains the quantifiers and proof.

Explore the results

proved

Prescribed finite dimensions and Holevo gap

An explicit finite size guarantee gives single-use and two-use bounds for the same channel, and hence a quantitative additivity gap.

proved

Operational classical capacity equals regularized Holevo information

The rate defined using physical codes equals the regularized Holevo information; the equality is proved rather than used as a definition.

proved

Input-qubit cost of the growing-K separation

A growing-K family makes single-use information vanish while two-use information and capacity grow, with explicit input-qubit costs.

proved

Free-group Collins-Youn bound

The free-group comparison bounds an operator norm by a Hilbert–Schmidt coefficient norm, setting the scale for the channel certificate.

proved

Weyl-extension Holevo equality at every positive tensor power

At every positive tensor power, the Weyl extension turns minimum output entropy into an exact Holevo quantity.

proved

A fixed channel has sublinear regrouped two-use gap

Regrouping more and more uses of one fixed channel cannot produce a persistent linear two-block gap.
