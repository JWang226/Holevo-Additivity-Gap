Quantum communication · mathematics and Lean proofs

How much could entanglement help in classical communication?

Entangling the inputs to repeated uses of a noisy quantum channel can improve the transmission of classical messages. This site explains an explicit finite-dimensional construction and its Lean proofs.

The communication benchmarks

\chi(T)Single-use Holevo information: optimize over ensembles of inputs to one use.

\tfrac12\chi(T\otimes T)Two-use Holevo information per use, allowing entangled inputs.

C(T)Operational classical capacity: the supremum of rates achievable with vanishing decoding error.

Main results

An unbounded two-use advantage

Two uses of the same channel can have arbitrarily many more bits of Holevo information than twice its single-use value. Entangled inputs produce this absolute gain.

Tiny single-use information, large capacity

Along one channel family, single-use Holevo information tends to zero while two-use information per use and operational classical capacity grow without bound. The capacity advantage and the relative two-use gain can be arbitrarily large simultaneously.

Explicit channel-size bounds

Holding a sufficiently large construction parameter fixed gives a family whose gap grows linearly with output-qubit cost and as the square root of input-qubit cost. The construction specifies finite dimensions and sufficient parameter thresholds.

These are families of channels: their dimensions grow as stronger separations are requested. Qubit cost means the base-two logarithm of the input or output dimension.
