exact endpoint

Uniform adjoint norm estimate at prescribed dimensions

proved

A finite Haar expectation estimate and net selection yield a uniform adjoint bound at the specified dimensions.

Mathematical content

At the prescribed local size and moment order, the finite Haar expected norm of each admissible self-adjoint linear polynomial is controlled by its free-regular norm with an explicit multiplicative error.

A finite net and a combined polynomial then select one sample whose block channel \Phi satisfies

\|\Phi^*(A)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le\frac{n+1}{n-1}\,c(K,n)\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}

for every traceless Hermitian output observable.

Two formal steps

The expectation theorem supplies the analytic comparison. The certificate theorem accepts that comparison as a premise and proves the finite sample-selection and net-extension consequences. Their combination in the main assembler removes the expectation premise.

The interactive proof map preserves this distinction: separately proved ingredients can meet in an assembler without one theorem's proof directly referencing the other.

Why uniformity matters

Testing a few observables would not control every output density matrix. The net reduction and extension make the estimate uniform, allowing the purity/entropy theorem to bound all single-use ensembles.

Read the adjoint-certificate concept for the trace-duality argument.

Formal scope and manuscript correspondence

The first theorem is a conditional assembly interface; the second discharges its ExplicitHaarExpectation hypothesis under the same numerical K,n conditions. Taken together they give the manuscript certificate unconditionally.
